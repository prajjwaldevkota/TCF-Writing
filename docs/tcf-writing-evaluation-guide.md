# TCF Canada writing evaluation guide

Merged from source checks dated 2026-10-17 and 2026-11-19. The facts below were not re-verified live for this merge. Recheck the official sources before shipping or relying on a rule for a real exam.

## A. Official public information

### A1. Format

TCF Canada Expression écrite lasts 60 minutes and contains three tasks.

| Task | Expected production | Published word range |
|---|---|---:|
| Tâche 1 | A message describing, recounting and/or explaining something to one or more identified recipients | 60–120 |
| Tâche 2 | An article, letter, note or similar text recounting an experience or event, with comments, opinions or arguments serving a purpose | 120–150 |
| Tâche 3 | A text comparing viewpoints from two documents on a social issue and giving the candidate’s position | 120–180 |

Candidates are expected to communicate clearly, provide requested information, describe, recount and explain, justify choices or positions, connect ideas coherently, express and argue an opinion, and use vocabulary and structures appropriate to the task.

### A2. Official correction process

Two evaluators independently assess written expression in a double-blind process. Each assigns each task a level from `A1 not achieved` to `C2`. The six judgments are combined into a final score and level. Sufficient disagreement can trigger a third correction. The combination formula and numerical domain weights are unpublished.

### A3. Official score conversion

IRCC maps the **final official TCF Canada writing score** to NCLC as follows (source check dated 2026-11-11).

| Final writing score (/20) | NCLC |
|---|---:|
| 4–5 | 4 |
| 6 | 5 |
| 7–9 | 6 |
| 10–11 | 7 |
| 12–13 | 8 |
| 14–15 | 9 |
| 16–20 | 10+ |

Use this table only with an official final ability score. It supplies neither a per-task conversion nor a CEFR-to-NCLC mapping.

### A4. Word count

The official sample defines a word as a set of signs between two spaces. Count whitespace-separated tokens, treating line breaks as separators. Keep apostrophes and hyphens within a token (`l’été` and `peut-être` each count as one).

**Simulator convention.** By default, exclude standalone tokens containing no letter or digit, such as a lone dash or exclamation mark. Include entered titles, greetings and signatures. If either count is within three words of either limit, label the count borderline and report both the simulator count and the count including all standalone signs.

## B. Pedagogical method

Everything in this section is a practice convention. Label practice rules explicitly in feedback.

### B1. Scoring limits

Do not invent official domain points, task marks, weights or an aggregation formula. Never derive a final /20 score from one task or any set of tasks. Never derive NCLC from one isolated task. Do not average task ranges or checklist entries. Store an official score only from an actual official result supplied by the learner.

### B2. Evidence domains

Use these headings to organize observations. They carry no point values.

| Domain | Evidence to consider |
|---|---|
| Linguistic | Vocabulary range and precision, grammatical control, spelling and punctuation, sentence construction and elaboration |
| Pragmatic | Task fulfilment and communicative outcome, coherence and cohesion, progression and thematic development, explanation, justification, comparison and argumentation where required |
| Sociolinguistic | Fit with the communication situation, relationship with the recipient, genre conventions, tone and register |

### B3. Two reading passes

One reader makes both passes. They do not simulate two independent official examiners.

1. **Communication.** Read without line editing. Check requested actions, clarity of the result, development and progression, coherence and cohesion, genre, recipient, tone and register. For Tâche 3, also check source use and comparison.
2. **Language control.** Examine vocabulary range, precision and collocation, grammar and sentence variety, spelling and punctuation. Explain how frequently errors recur and what effect they have on the reader.

Give a holistic range such as `B1–B2` supported by quotations. Explain what limits the range and select at most five priorities.

### B4. Preliminary admissibility-risk check

Flag each risk with evidence. Never declare an official outcome. Check for a missing task, an off-topic response, absent requested actions, a word-range violation, copying from the prompt or sources, or evidence that a memorized or plagiarized text replaces the requested response. Never accuse a learner without evidence. Assess illegible handwriting only when handwriting is available. Distinguish a missing learner response from a source the simulator failed to supply.

### B5. Practice descriptors for boundary decisions

Judge what the learner achieves in context. No particular connector or grammatical construction is required. Use the full A1–C2 scale when the evidence warrants it.

**A2.** Short clauses carry basic information through simple links such as *et*, *mais* or *parce que*. Limited elaboration and recurring agreement or tense errors can coexist with a comprehensible message. Check whether basic politeness suits the recipient.

**B1.** A connected, largely linear account of a familiar situation conveys an opinion and basic reasons. Look for usable everyday vocabulary, shifts between background and events where relevant, and sufficient register control. Repetition or circumlocution can limit precision. Clean spelling alone does not establish B2.

**B2.** A clear, developed message lets the reader follow relationships between claims. Look for controlled subordination, varied reference and vocabulary, and sustained register. Relative clauses, conditions, concessions and the subjunctive provide evidence when they serve the meaning. Occasional slips can coexist with B2. On Tâche 3, distinguish genuine comparison from unrelated summaries.

**C1.** Precise collocations, flexible syntax and economical reformulation express distinctions that matter to the reader. Look for nuance, concession or a response to a counterargument where appropriate, consistent register and few systematic errors. A memorized expression or a rare tense alone establishes no level.

### B6. Task-specific checks and practice caps

Both caps below apply **only to the task-fulfilment judgment**. Assess language separately. When applying a cap, call it a “practice rule” and identify the omitted detail or missing function. FEI has not published these caps or numerical task weights.

**Tâche 1.** Is the reason for writing immediately clear? Audit each requested detail and action. Check whether the opening, tone, tu/vous and closing suit the recipient and whether the message describes, recounts or explains as requested. Under the practice rule, an omitted explicitly requested detail caps task fulfilment at B1. A short, flawless routine message gives limited evidence for advanced proficiency.

**Tâche 2.** Look for an identifiable event or experience with progression, concrete on-topic details, and a distinction between background and events where relevant. Check whether the commentary serves the stated purpose and whether genre, readership, title (where appropriate), tone and ending fit together. Under the practice rule, when commentary is required and the response is entirely narrative, task fulfilment is capped at B1.

**Tâche 3.** Use the prompt and both documents. Check faithful coverage of each document separately, the relationship drawn between the viewpoints, and reformulation in the learner’s own words. Look for a clear personal position developed through reasons, consequences or examples, logical progression and a compatible conclusion.

Identify two functions even where paragraphs overlap. A neutral reformulation relating the views typically takes 40–60 words, followed by an argued personal stance typically taking 80–120. Apply part lengths only where specified by the assignment. A particular layout, exactly two arguments and specific reporting verbs are not universal requirements. Without both documents, correct the language and withhold the complete holistic range because source fidelity and comparison cannot be verified.

## C. Required feedback wording

Use the configured feedback language. For single-task and targeted practice, include:

- `Pedagogical level range for this task: ...`
- `This assessment is not official.`
- `One isolated task cannot determine the final /20 score or NCLC level.`

When relevant, add `Admissibility risk: ...` on its own line with the supporting evidence.

## D. Optional full-mock NCLC projection

This feature was added at the learner’s explicit request.

**Eligibility.** Before calling the model, the application checks that all three responses are present and in range, all task instructions are supplied, and both Tâche 3 documents are present. Pass the computed eligibility flag to the model. The model must not decide eligibility. Isolated tasks and targeted practice never receive a projection.

**Method.** Estimate a separate, broad NCLC writing range holistically across the three tasks. Never average task ranges, use an official-result field, or imply an official scoring formula. Material task-fulfilment risks can justify withholding even when the inputs are mechanically complete. Give the reason.

**Interpreting Tâche 3.** It gives the strongest opportunity to demonstrate synthesis and sustained argument. If Tâches 1 and 2 look B2 but Tâche 3 shows only B1 development, explain the conflict and widen the range or withhold an unsupported NCLC 7+ estimate.

**Full-mock feedback.** Retain the non-official assessment statement and any individual risk lines from section C. Identify each task and its range or withholding reason. Use the following wording in the configured language:

- `Pedagogical level range for each task: ...`
- `NCLC writing projection: NCLC X–Y` or `NCLC writing projection: withheld: <reason>.`
- `This is an uncalibrated AI readiness projection. It is not an official result and not the product of an official scoring formula.`
- `Only an official TCF Canada result determines the final /20 score and NCLC level.`

## Writing descriptors for the two bounds

These short practice paraphrases draw on the Council of Europe’s written assessment grid and its scales for grammatical accuracy and sociolinguistic appropriateness. Use the pertinent features in context. Explain the evidence for each endpoint and the best-supported level. [CEFR Companion Volume, chapters 5 and appendix 4](https://rm.coe.int/cefr-companion-volume-with-new-descriptors-2020/16809ea0d4)

| Level | Language control | Coherence and purpose | Recipient and register |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Familiar words and rehearsed patterns | Isolated simple statements | Basic greetings |
| A2 | Basic patterns with recurring errors | Short linked clauses convey everyday needs | Routine polite requests |
| B1 | Familiar structures usually convey the intended meaning | A linear account gives reasons or describes events | Common social conventions |
| B2 | Sufficient variety and control to develop viewpoints | Relevant detail and explicit relationships support an argument | Adjusts formality to the situation |
| C1 | Flexible, precise expression with occasional slips | Integrates supporting points into a structured account | Adapts style to audience and purpose |
| C2 | Sustained control of complex expression | Guides the reader through nuanced distinctions | Handles subtle implications of register |

**Synthetic teaching excerpts.** These excerpts illustrate individual features. They have no independent examiner labels and must never be quoted as learner evidence.

- **Tâche 1, inviting a neighbour.** “Venez samedi. On mange chez moi.” conveys the invitation. “Seriez-vous libre samedi vers midi ? Si les escaliers vous gênent, je peux réserver la salle du rez-de-chaussée.” adds actionable detail and adjusts the invitation to the recipient. The conditional alone does not determine the level.
- **Tâche 2, reviewing a repair workshop.** “Nous avons réparé mon vélo. J’ai aimé cet atelier parce que les gens étaient gentils.” gives an event and a reason. “Quand la chaîne a sauté de nouveau, l’animatrice m’a laissé chercher la panne. C’est ce temps d’essai qui rend l’atelier utile aux débutants pressés de devenir autonomes.” uses an episode to explain a judgment for the intended readership.

**Examiner-commented reference for B2.** In the DELF pilot letter requesting remote work, the examiner identifies B2 language control despite agreement and pronoun errors. The same letter misses a requested practical argument and sometimes addresses the director too directly. When a learner shows a similar profile, explain language control, task fulfilment and register separately. Use the original TCF assignment for its word limits.
